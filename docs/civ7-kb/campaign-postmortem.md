# Catherine / Maya / Maya / Russia: postmortem

## Outcome and limits

**OBSERVED:** at Modern **T56**, Hatshepsut had **360 Tourism**, we had **148**, the native cultural target was **296**, and her five-turn countdown was active with zero turns accumulated. The user chose to let the run go and consider a Modern T1 replay. **We did not observe the final defeat screen.**

This is the conceded run's record, not a loaded save reconstruction. Historical numbers are useful for diagnosis; they are not live advice.

## What the user wanted

- Peaceful science with Catherine; relaxed, flavorful choices rather than relentless optimization.
- Maya continuity across the first two Ages, then Russia for variety. Separate Modern Maya and Russia saves were created.
- Wonders were part of the fun: White Tower, Oxford and Hermitage were meaningful successes.
- No unwanted diplomatic/cultural victory pivot. A modest Tourism defense was acceptable.
- At concession, willingness to try a more aggressive approach; this did not automatically authorize war or a full new campaign.

## Empire and transition choices

**OBSERVED / checkpoint record:** Exploration T140 was saved before transition. The capital moved from Copan to Divodurum, renamed **St. Petersburg**. Copan remained a city; **Bàakʼ** was restored as the third city for **800 gold**, not the earlier 200 quote before the final selection. Kyiv and Roskilde had been incorporated before transition. Isfahan and Ava were deliberately left independent in Exploration.

The opening intentionally kept **three cities**, because the owned Scientific attribute's advertised specialist benefit would be weaker with a fourth city. This was a **campaign perk constraint**, not a universal rule that Civ VII always favors three cities.

Mementos: **Eagle Banner + Antikythera Mechanism**. Modern Dedications: **On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres**, **Da Ming Lu**, **Let's Calicut a Deal**. Elective Republic was adopted at T2. Thirteen towns plus three cities gave 16 settlements after founding Nijni-Novgorod and Astrakhan.

## What worked

- **Oxford completed T17 in Bàakʼ**, beating America. The notification repeated Industrialization; Rail Stations unlocked and Industrialization II stopped appearing as a choice. A free base-plus-Mastery interpretation is plausible, not experimentally established.
- Schoolhouses/Laboratories and Russian science/production Traditions created a real science economy. Reported science was roughly 487/turn at T48, versus about 377 at T17.
- Mining focus, resource allocation and trade improved income enough to support targeted purchases.
- Rail connection scoring appeared in the later Tourism ledger: Monastery and Obshchina entries showed +3 rail-related points alongside their earlier +1 entries.
- Radio Telescope became available after Radio II and a Radio Station; it finished by the T51 observations.
- Uluru excavation succeeded. At T42, the **Wondrous** narrative choice explicitly offered **+1 Artifact** on Close. Later the exhibit appeared in StP's Palace and the ledger recorded +5 Artifact Tourism.
- Hermitage finished T53 and the ledger explicitly recorded **+12 Tourism**.
- The user found and corrected several practical issues through the UI instead of accepting tool extraction blindly.

## Why the strategy failed to prevent the countdown

**INFERRED diagnosis:** the key failure was global timing, not one bad placement.

1. We optimized a three-city research engine while Hatshepsut already had a large inherited Tourism lead.
2. We repeatedly reported current-tier buffers without turning the next multiplier drop into a concrete development deadline.
3. We recommended many individually reasonable infrastructure builds, research detours and an optional Wonder without demonstrating that their combined payoff arrived before that deadline.
4. Archaeology began too late to rely on ordinary competitive dig sites. A suggested Natural History II switch was not actually made until later; the advisor failed to keep recommendation and execution distinct.
5. Aerodromes were started late. At T56, Bàakʼ still quoted nine turns; even an immediate expected +6 Tourism was far short of the +33 needed for a conservative interruption at Hatshepsut's then-current score.
6. We never produced a credible combined Innovation + Launch Pad finish forecast early enough to compare with rival dominance. High science alone was treated too reassuringly.

At T53, 355 versus a target of 444 looked safe at 3x. Under the upcoming 2x tier, **the same 148 score implied a target of 296**, already below her score. A larger current buffer was not necessarily improving future safety.

## Advice mistakes worth keeping, not hiding

- "No countdown yet" was reported as reassurance instead of a prompt to stress-test the next tier.
- Purchase advice changed too abruptly: Bàakʼ's Ironworks was first discouraged, then endorsed at essentially the same budget. The useful explanation was the cash-reserve versus saved-turns trade-off.
- Coordinates were offered without usable landmarks; some later cardinal directions were not established from the user's map.
- Ordinary ruins and Natural Wonder excavation were conflated during the crowded-site discussion. The screenshots/tooltip did not establish the exact disabled-action cause.
- Social Science was initially discussed as if it would replace Alchemy directly. The actual unlocked card occupied an **ordinary policy slot**; later advice corrected this.
- Null `overbuilds` was nearly read as an empty tile despite a settlement-record Obshchina.
- Several future queues were proposed as if completion or a research switch would naturally follow. The user remained the actor; suggested does not mean done.
- "Peaceful rescue not verified" at T56 was appropriate, but it came after weeks of in-game development that should have been evaluated against the deadline.

## What not to conclude

- This does **not** prove culture is always the strongest victory, science is impossible, Russia was the wrong choice, or war was mandatory.
- It does **not** prove a Modern T1 replay can win peacefully. That is an untested strategy proposal.
- It does **not** prove every infrastructure investment was individually bad. Their combined opportunity cost was not controlled against the rival's win timing.
- Do not hardcode T56 into future games. Query Age progress and applicable schedule rows.
