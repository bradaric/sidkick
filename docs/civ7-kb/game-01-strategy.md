# Game 01: build the win before the emergency

Reusable Civilization VII strategy and checkpoints, learned from Catherine's
**Maya → Maya → Russia** attempt and the **Exploration-age replay → Maya → Maya →
Prussia** route. Compiled **2026-11-16** from the campaign conversation and the
linked knowledge-base records.

## How to use this next time

Read the campaign comparison and checkpoint table before planning the next game.
Then use the relevant sections at research, production, policy, diplomacy and war
decisions. Refresh the loaded save before giving live advice: none of the scores,
coordinates, queues or recommendations here are current instructions.

The replay used **Test of Time, version 1.5.1 (1327230)**. The first attempt's full
settings were not archived. Starting Age, previous Ages, game speed, difficulty,
patches and campaign choices can change requirements, costs and scoring. Use native
targets and legal-action checks over remembered rules or quotes.

Evidence labels:

- **OBSERVED:** recorded tool output or user/UI observation, with its campaign and
  turn identified. Not necessarily a controlled experiment.
- **USER-REPORTED:** campaign history supplied by the player where no independent
  save comparison was performed.
- **INFERRED:** arithmetic or strategic judgment with stated assumptions.
- **UNVERIFIED:** a material uncertainty, not a fact to repeat as a game rule.

This is a decision framework, not a universal build order. The aim is a relaxed,
flavorful game with a credible victory plan, not optimizing away enjoyable Wonders
and military adventures.

## 1. Two attempts, and what changed

### Attempt A: Maya → Maya → Russia — we recognized the danger too late

**OBSERVED:** the Russia run was conceded at Modern **T56** with Hatty on **360
Tourism**, us on **148**, and her native cultural target at **296**. The newly
applicable **2×** threshold at **40% Age progress** had started her five-turn
countdown. A final defeat screen was **not observed**.

The warning existed before the countdown. At T53, our 148 Tourism versus Hatty's
355 looked safe against the then-current target of 444. But at a future 2× tier,
the same rival score would already qualify against us. At T56, the conservative
frozen-score defensive floor was 181: **33 more Tourism**, not another small yield
improvement.

Good local development was not enough. We built a real research economy, completed
Wonders and found Artifacts, but did not combine them into a timely survival and
Science-finish schedule. Archaeology and Aerodrome options arrived too late to
reliably close the actual deficit. "No countdown yet" became reassurance when it
should have triggered preparation for the next multiplier.

See the [original Russia postmortem](campaign-postmortem.md) for the dated record.
This does not establish that Russia, peaceful play or Science were inherently bad
choices.

### Attempt B: replay Exploration, then choose Prussia in Modern

**USER-REPORTED:** the successful route replayed the **Exploration Age**, rather
than merely restarting Modern T1. Record that distinction: there was more time to
reshape the inherited position. We did not reconstruct every Exploration decision
or isolate the effect of changing civilization in a controlled comparison.

**OBSERVED in Modern:** we kept Divodurum, Bàakʼ and Copan as the three-City core,
developed trade/resources and rail infrastructure, balanced GDP against Tourism,
and selectively modernized the military. That balance continued throughout the
game; economic and Tourism defense were not disposable preliminaries to Science.

A limited naval campaign worked better than our land fighting. Battleships helped
take Nekhen; Apia was also retained, and peace added Zau for **300 Influence**.
The player reported that Hatty won land engagements. We did not have grounds to
generalize naval success into superiority for an inland campaign. Ashoka later
held Swenett, which we had considered as an alternative peace concession.

After peace, we concentrated on research conversion, project policies, the Launch
Pad and a point-efficient combination of technologies and projects.

pre-victory state could be inspected. The last native read still said `won=false`
at T95. A subsequent player-provided screenshot explicitly shows **SCIENCE

win. The earlier native read and later victory screen describe successive states,
not a contradiction.

### The combined lesson

> Build recurring GDP, add timely Tourism, preserve military credibility, and
> concentrate on the preferred finish when that balanced position supports it.

The successful difference was not just "more Science" or "pick Prussia". It was
planning the whole race early enough, adapting to evidence and controlling the
opportunity cost of development and war.

## 2. Pursue a victory while financing survival

Keep four questions separate:

1. What wins us the game?
2. What could let someone else win first?
3. What is the next bottleneck in our plan?
4. What optional things do we want because they are enjoyable?

A Wonder can be fun and part of the defensive scoring plan. A military investment
can protect a Science game without making it a conquest campaign. The mistake is
not taking a detour: it is taking several without checking their combined timing.

### Read the entire victory board

| Victory | Inspect |
|---|---|
| Science | Innovation, research/building/project prerequisites, Pad condition and countdown |
| Economic | Accumulated GDP, recent scoring pace, leader/runner-up ratio |
| Cultural | Tourism totals, scoring opportunities and dominance ratio |
| Military/Dominion | Native Dominion points, leader/runner-up ratio and countdown |
| Other enabled conditions | Their loaded requirements and current state |

Dominion deserved more attention in our review. At the last checkpoint we led
**84–64**, a ratio of **1.3125×**: insufficient at 1.5×, but sufficient under a
hypothetical 1.25× tier if the scores stayed unchanged. That was an alternative
worth monitoring, not proof of an immediate win or justification to force more
conquest when the player preferred Science. Dominion points are not a count of
units, nor necessarily a one-point-per-settlement measure.

### Do not confuse yields with victory points

| Quantity | Purpose; what it is not |
|---|---|
| Science/turn | Researches technology; not Innovation |
| Innovation | Science-victory points; not a rival-relative research-yield race |
| Culture/turn | Researches civics; not Tourism |
| Tourism | Cultural dominance score; not automatically a per-turn yield |
| Gold income/treasury | Purchases, upkeep and reserves; not GDP |
| GDP | Economic score from specific trackers; not all economic yields |
| Army size | Military capability; not automatically Dominion progress |

## 3. Plan for the next dominance threshold

**"No countdown" is a status report, not a survival plan.**

Our loaded full-campaign Modern schedule was:

| Modern Age progress | Multiplier |
|---|---:|
| Before 20% | 4× |
| 20% | 3× |
| 40% | 2× |
| 60% | 1.5× |
| 80% | 1.25× |

Use the next campaign's **native schedule, targets and countdowns**, not this table
blindly. These are Age-progress percentages, not universal turn numbers. T56 in
the Russia run and T84 in the Prussia run were observations, not interchangeable
deadlines. T80 was not a verified Science-finish date or automatic defeat date.

For a rival score `L` and future multiplier `m`, a conservative defensive floor is:

```text
required_score = floor(L / m) + 1
gap = max(0, required_score - our_score)
```

This assumes we are or become the relevant runner-up and deliberately avoids an
equality boundary. The UI's native target and rounding take precedence. Another
runner-up can also set the defensive bar.

At each review, calculate:

- The current position and actual countdowns.
- Today's scores under the next lower multiplier.
- Whether committed defensive gains arrive in time, with a margin for rival gains.

GDP has recurring scoring ticks; Tourism can arrive in bursts. A frozen-score
calculation is a baseline, not a prediction that the rival will stop developing.
Graph axes matter: a falling **share of accumulated world GDP** does not mean a
player's accumulated GDP has fallen.

Safety means surviving until our intended finish, not necessarily making every
later threshold harmless forever. If a rival countdown is already active, pause
routine infrastructure advice and calculate a credible timely response; see
[victories and deadlines](victories-and-deadlines.md).

## 4. Checkpoints: review early, not just at a warning

| Checkpoint | Questions to settle |
|---|---|
| Early Antiquity | What supports the leader? Which territory, resources and defensive positions matter? |
| Before each transition | What survives, what scores carry forward, and what next-Age options are being prepared? |
| Early Exploration | Are inherited scoring deficits forming? Which exploration, trade or scoring opportunities need early access? |
| Modern opening | What is the complete preferred finish, and which rival lead threatens it? |
| Before each multiplier drop | Would anyone qualify at the next tier today? What closes the gap in time? |
| Before a long build/research detour | Does it address survival, shorten the finish, or have an explicit optional purpose? |
| Before war | Objective, force, access, support, economic exposure and acceptable peace terms? |
| After a major unlock | What is now legal, and what still needs construction, a policy slot or project production? |
| At 100 Innovation | Pad condition, competing victories and countdown protection—not extra Innovation by habit |
| Before the final click | Record actual points, prerequisites, countdown and competing threats |

Use occasional turn-number reviews as convenient reminders, not substitutes for
Age-progress checks. Modern T50 was useful in this game because it prompted a
strategic review while there was still time to change course.

## 5. Work backward from the complete Science finish

Do not stop at "we have excellent Science". Maintain this ledger:

| Requirement | Record |
|---|---|
| Innovation | Current points and points still needed |
| Research | Exact base technologies/Masteries, rewards and current quotes |
| Infrastructure | Required building in the producing City |
| Placement | Legal site, terrain and everything displaced |
| Projects | Cost, current quote and point reward |
| Limits | Repeatable, once per civilization, or other restrictions |
| Countdown | Native duration and ongoing prerequisites |
| Rival pressure | What must be prevented before our route finishes? |

### Read every unlock, and separate research from production

In this replay:

- Rocketry itself awarded **5 Innovation**, as well as unlocking infrastructure
  and projects. Missing that award initially complicated our plan unnecessarily.
- Flight II unlocked Trans-Oceanic Flight; researching it did not award the
  project's five points.
- Research Initiative generated Science while active, not Innovation on completion.
- The repeatable project was **Crewed Space Flight Preparation**, not an old-style
  project description claiming an immediate Science victory.

Our eventual exact-100 route was:

| Source | Innovation |
|---|---:|
| Total after Rocketry and Combustion II | 71 |
| Aerodynamics II | +5 |
| Radio II | +2 |
| Armor II | +2 |
| One Crewed Space Flight Preparation | +15 |
| Trans-Oceanic Flight | +5 |
| **Total, reached T91** | **100** |

This avoided a second Preparation and did not require Satellite for the point
total. It is an **observed recipe for this position**, not a universal best order.
It supplies later evidence for some questions left unresolved by the Russia run's
[older science notes](modern-science.md) and [corrections](open-questions-and-corrections.md).

### Identify the critical path

Ask: **is the next delay research, building construction, project production or
the countdown?** Spend and assign Cities accordingly.

- While Rocketry was the bottleneck, supporting Cities ran Research Initiative.
- Once the Pad was available, the capital built it while research continued.
- Afterward, the capital produced point projects while Masteries supplied the
  remaining Innovation in parallel.
- Once the required technologies were complete, continuing research was no longer
  necessary for that finish route.

Recheck queues after completions. In our observed play, Research Initiative entries
cleared and needed attention; its displayed turn quote was not an Innovation
award or proof it would stay running indefinitely.

### One Pad can be enough

Our native requirement was **100 Innovation, an undamaged Launch Pad and five
qualifying countdown turns**. One Pad could produce repeated Preparations.

Consider a second Pad only if its construction plus parallel project completion
beats the first City's remaining project schedule. More infrastructure does not
automatically produce an earlier win. Additional Pads do not shorten the native
countdown.

Forecasts are conditional. At T82, quotes suggested about 12 production turns to
qualify, then five countdown turns. We qualified nine turns later at T91. That
was about three turns faster than the rough budget, not proof quotes always
overestimate. Refresh timings after research, growth, cards and production changes.

## 6. GDP: recurring scoring, not just emergency insurance

GDP was a sustained strategic investment. Evaluate each economic action by:

1. Its recurring GDP contribution.
2. Its ordinary yields, resources and capacity.
3. Its opportunity cost before the intended finish.

Rail Stations, Ports, Factories, assignments and trade can contribute differently.
Do not value them solely by the Gold number in a yield preview.

### Resource checklist

- What resource do we gain, and is it newly useful or replacing an existing import?
- Can it legally be assigned where it helps?
- Is there capacity? What would be displaced?
- Does it require a Factory or network connection?
- Does it support Science, Production, Happiness or scoring?
- When do we expect it to affect an actual scoring tick?

Owning a resource is not the same as activating its benefit. Extra capacity is
useful only with resources worth assigning. Factory resource types and requirements
need checking separately from ordinary resource slots.

### Rail and purchase measurement

- A station's presence is not proof of rail-network membership.
- Route-based scoring is not necessarily a station count.
- Town Production converts to Gold; it is not sent to the capital's projects.
- A large Gold-income preview is not an equally large GDP increase.

**OBSERVED:** Chichen Itza's station cost 4,032 Gold. The first recorded interval
after purchase showed **60 GDP**, versus the prior **58** rate. Gold-building
scoring rose by two; rail-category scoring remained at ten. This was consistent
with a **+2 marginal GDP rate in that interval**, not the conditional +4 estimate.
It was not a controlled experiment guaranteeing every later station's effect.

Copan's earlier station coincided with a different scoring change. Do not copy
that result onto another settlement without evidence.

Record category totals before and after a normal scoring interval, the action's
timing, and other intervening changes. Do not spend merely to manufacture a test
unless the user explicitly wants that experiment.

## 7. Tourism: prepare pipelines before the deficit becomes urgent

Our defense used several sources rather than a single rescue button.

```text
Archaeology:
research → Explorer → eligible research/access → travel → excavation
→ narrative reward → display → verified score

Aerodrome bonus:
required Mastery → qualifying retained buildings → legal Aerodrome
→ preserved assets → verified score

Rail bonus:
required research → qualifying Unique Improvement types
→ actual connection → verified score
```

The unlock, building or unit alone is not the completed scoring pipeline.

- Empty Great Work slots are not displayed works.
- A Museum is not an emergency solution without a timely work to fill it.
- Ordinary ruins and Natural Wonder excavation have different prerequisites.
- A pending narrative may contain the reward; excavation is not the last step.
- Flight II's Unique Building bonus differs from rail's Unique Improvement bonus.
- Buildings, Unique Buildings, Unique Quarters and Unique Improvements are distinct.
- Culture buildings, Cultural Festivals and Hinder Civic Study are not direct
  substitutes for Tourism.
- Capturing a settlement does not establish that a rival's accumulated Tourism
  disappears.

Eiffel was a legitimate dual-purpose choice: enjoyable and part of the Tourism
position, not merely Culture. Still compare the full build time with Science and
defensive deadlines. Verify the award rather than attributing every contemporaneous
Tourism change to one building.

## 8. City roles, Town focuses and future placement space

Assign core Cities clear jobs:

- A primary project City with Production, support and suitable future building sites.
- Research-support Cities with useful infrastructure and temporary conversion.
- Towns supplying resources, Gold, food support and strategic coverage.

This is a role framework, not a fixed City count. Our three-City core reflected
campaign-specific perks; do not generalize it to every leader or ruleset.

### Preserve sites before filling the map

Before adding another building or Wonder, identify future full-tile needs and an
acceptable replacement district. Preserve a fallback site and account for
specialists, Happiness, resources and unique assets.

**OBSERVED:** the Launch Pad UI named Pavilion as the overbuild, but its full tile
also held a Hospital. After construction started, the old paired district was no
longer listed. A single named replacement is not complete loss accounting.

### Specialize Towns by their actual inventory

- Mining: count qualifying improvements; the Production becomes Town Gold.
- Fishing/Farming: check connected City recipients and food needs; not direct
  Innovation or guaranteed faster research.
- Urban Center: count native qualifying Quarters; do not rely on the name alone.
- Hub: count useful connections, not all nearby settlements.
- Resort: verify terrain and scoring requirements before promising Tourism.

Specialization stopped ordinary local growth in our observed system. Late in the
finish, the next population being 20-plus turns away made immediate benefits more
attractive. Earlier, keep growth if it secures a valuable resource or territory.

Our late examples were Nekhen Fishing, Xunantunich Mining and Zau Fishing. These
were support choices, not direct Innovation engines. Xunantunich's reported only
connection was Köln, so extra food there did not have a verified core-City recipient.

## 9. Policies: slot constraints and timing matter

For each unlock:

1. Identify the actual slot group and capacity.
2. Identify the displaced card, or a better arrangement of flexible slots.
3. Compare the net preview and every core City's upkeep/Happiness.
4. Match the bonus to what the Cities are actually producing.

Our progression used specialist and Town Science, upkeep support, project bonuses,
and later displayed-work Science. Public Works moved from a flexible Tradition
slot to a newly available ordinary slot, allowing Milpa to return. Preservation
Societies subsequently replaced Milpa as a better candidate in that position.

Do not remove a support card merely because it does not say "Science": it may
enable the specialist economy. Likewise, do not compare base card descriptions
as if they were a complete swap simulation.

Distinguish **project**, **building**, **Wonder** and **military-unit** Production.
Public Works and Think-tanks helped the projects, not Launch Pad construction
itself. Recheck actual project quotes; do not invent multiplicative stacking.

## 10. Military and diplomacy: limited objectives, an exit plan

Our fleet's success did not establish land superiority. That distinction helped
keep a limited success from becoming an expensive inland war.

### Before declaring war

Write down the objective, legal capture method, revealed access, visible enemies
and fogged uncertainty, force health, upgrades, commander logistics, war support,
Happiness/trade exposure and acceptable exit terms.

For an island target, check actual bombardment and capture mechanics. Do not
import Civ6 naval-melee assumptions. A Commander does not automatically confer
an unpurchased amphibious promotion.

### During war and peace talks

- Preserve damaged units and evaluate local capability, not just empire-wide counts.
- Treat war support as an empire-management issue before widespread unhappiness.
- Reassess whether another conquest is worth delaying the preferred finish.
- Compare offered settlements at equal prices by resources, position, defensibility,
  integration burden and what we must surrender.
- A peace invitation does not guarantee reparations or acceptance of a demand.
- Transferring a former city-state to a major is not liberation.

Zau for Influence, while retaining Nekhen and Apia, secured a limited result.
Ashoka later acquiring Swenett made that particular choice look good; it does not
prove the outcome could have been guaranteed in advance.

### Spend Influence for a defined purpose

| Action | Decision test |
|---|---|
| Collaboration | Does our added output matter before the finish? |
| Hindering | Which specific rival capability or timing are we delaying? |
| Counter Spy | Which valuable research or infrastructure are we protecting? |
| War support | What support imbalance and associated strain need addressing? |

Counter Spy is insurance, not immunity. We observed Hatty's exposed espionage and
a +90 Influence notification while it was active; we did not prove Counter Spy
caused detection or that the hostile mission failed. Units do not provide a spy
shield merely by standing on a Launch Pad.

Monitor alliance changes and agendas. Sharing an enemy does not erase ideological
or other grievances. José's official Kapwa agenda dislikes leaders starting the
most active Sanctions; our sanctions against Hatty could therefore be relevant,
but unlabeled relationship amounts did not establish an exact causal breakdown.

## 11. Gold: spend for a purpose, not because we can

Before buying, finish this sentence:

> This purchase improves our chances before the finish because…

Good answers: repairs a critical asset; closes a verified score gap; changes a
relevant completion time; protects against a credible threat; buys an explicitly
desired optional experience without endangering the plan.

Weak answers: affordable; larger yield number; habitual build; might help eventually.

Keep a reserve for identifiable contingencies, not an arbitrary number. But do
not hoard automatically either: compare its purpose with the purchase's actual
benefit. In this campaign, a Fighter was optional insurance; the expensive station
was an economic investment. Neither was a demonstrated faster Science finish.

## 12. Evidence and forecasting discipline

- A recommendation, submitted order, queued action, completion and victory are
  different states. Confirm each where consequential.
- Read current costs and availability, not old prices or base definitions.
- A complete snapshot is not atomic. Compare turns when the player is advancing.
- Missing data is unknown, not zero. No visible enemies does not establish an
  empty world; peace does not guarantee no future attack.
- Tool/UI contradictions should be named, not smoothed into certainty.
- Queued travel is not arrival. Do not blindly duplicate interrupted/unknown orders.
- Detection is not proof of failed espionage; conquest is not proof of erased scores.
- Countdown turns are conditional; do not promise victory on an exact future date.
- Do not assume loss of dominance resets a countdown; inspect native pause/resume
  state and applicable rules.

The Russia attempt was conceded, not observed through a defeat screen. The Prussia
Science victory was subsequently confirmed by the player's victory-screen
screenshot; T95 remains the last verified numerical checkpoint, not a verified
final victory turn. Keeping these evidence boundaries explicit makes the strategic
lessons more useful, not less.

## 14. What paid off, and what to improve

| Replay measure | Modern T52 | Last verified T95 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Our GDP | 4,126 | 6,601 |
| Hatty's GDP lead | 1.690× | 1.287× |
| Our Tourism | 199 | 263 |
| Hatty's Tourism lead | 1.487× | 1.331× |
| Our Innovation | 64 | 100 |

**Paid off:** sustained GDP development; timely Tourism additions; preparing for
the falling thresholds; selective naval modernization; ending a limited war;
productive peace terms; policy rearrangement; research/production overlap; and an
exact Innovation ledger instead of building every space project.

**Improve next time:** identify inherited danger in Exploration, budget the entire
finish earlier, monitor Dominion as well, preserve space for full-tile buildings,
measure expensive purchases before calling their marginal scoring return certain,
and keep enjoyable detours explicit rather than letting them accumulate invisibly.

Neither attempt proves that one civilization, military approach or victory path is
universally best. The transferable advantage was **planning the whole race**.

### Carry these rules into the next game

1. Check every victory condition.
2. Stress-test the next threshold before it arrives.
3. Balance GDP and Tourism throughout development.
4. Work backward from the complete finish, not a yield target.
5. Read base and Mastery rewards carefully.
6. Parallelize research and production.
7. Count full scoring pipelines and full placement losses.
8. Measure marginal results and label estimates honestly.
9. Define a limited war's exit before entering it.
10. Stop investing in things that cannot matter before the finish.
11. Keep enjoyable choices, with their opportunity cost explicit.
12. Record what was actually observed, not just the ending we expect.

**Core lesson: build a strong enough position across the board that the preferred
victory becomes a controlled finish—not a last-minute rescue.**
